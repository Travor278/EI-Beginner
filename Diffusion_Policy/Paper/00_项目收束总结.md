negative results 和后续研究素材归档，而不是继续消耗同一套算力与 benchmark。

## Negative Results Worth Keeping

### 1. Hand-Crafted Difficulty Was Not Enough

The early curriculum idea used surface-level trajectory statistics such as length
and action variance. The result showed that this did not capture the real contact
or manipulation difficulty. This is a useful failed direction because it narrows
future work away from naive ordering rules.

### 2. Hardness Reweighting Was Too Weak

The predictive-hardness pipeline worked technically, but using the signal only as
chunk-level sampling/reweighting did not improve control. The later interpretation
is that world-model signals, if useful, probably need to enter representation or
loss design rather than only the data sampler.

### 3. Frozen Visual Latents Were Not Action-Sensitive Enough

The v3.5 world-model probe did not beat a trivial copy baseline in DINO latent
space. This suggests the chosen visual representation was too stable/global for
single-step action-conditioned dynamics in Push-T.

### 4. Push-T and Current Lift Became Bad Gates

Push-T reached a training ceiling and had noisy test rollouts. Robomimic Lift in
the tested configuration also saturated, with both baseline and method variants
reaching perfect scores. These benchmarks, under the current evaluation settings,
cannot support a strong method claim.

## Why We Stop Here

The project hit three practical limits at the same time:

- Compute and GPU budget limited how far larger benchmarks or broader seeds could
  be explored.
- Push-T became too saturated/noisy to distinguish subtle method changes.
- Robomimic Lift in the tested setting was too easy, so it did not solve the gate
  problem.

Continuing to run the same matrix would mostly produce more ambiguous numbers.
The scientifically cleaner choice is to close the thread and preserve the evidence.

## What This Folder Should Be Used For

- As a reproducibility record for Diffusion Policy baseline learning.
- As a debugging reference for modern GPU compatibility and WSL setup.
- As a compact archive of negative results around DP curriculum, hardness, world
  models, and frequency supervision.
- As a starting point only if a future project changes the benchmark and compute
  assumptions.

## What Should Not Be Claimed

- Do not claim SFC-DP robustly improves over Diffusion Policy.
- Do not claim DLOS-DP passed its world-model gate.
- Do not claim Push-T alone is enough evidence for a publishable new method.
- Do not continue the same Push-T/Lift setting as if more epochs would settle it.

## Future Resume Conditions

A future restart should satisfy at least one of these conditions before spending
more compute:

- Use a harder or less saturated benchmark, such as Robomimic Can or LIBERO.
- Use a low-data regime where baseline performance is not already near ceiling.
- Increase evaluation episodes and seeds enough to reduce rollout noise.
- Use action-sensitive state or object-centric representations instead of frozen
  global visual latents.
